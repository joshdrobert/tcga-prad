Aim: Accurate postoperative risk stratification in localized prostate cancer is vital for identifying candidates for salvage therapy and emerging theranostic staging. Multiple studies report discrimination gains from prognostic gene signatures. We evaluated whether apparent genomic gains in TCGA-PRAD reflect authentic biology or artifacts of unadjudicated endpoint incompleteness.

Material & Methods: Multi-source clinical forms were audited across 314 TCGA-PRAD radical prostatectomy patients. A definitive recurrence cohort (n=261; 70 events, 191 double-negative censored) and a paired molecular subcohort with verified 13-gene RNA expression (n=187; 44 events, 143 non-events) were evaluated using stratified 5-fold cross-validation. We evaluated counterfactual naive extraction relying solely on initial single-encounter biochemical recurrence (BCR) forms versus harmonized multi-source adjudication incorporating secondary new tumor events (NTE). Models included clinical staging (pathologic T/N stage, surgical margins, Gleason score, age) with and without the 13-gene panel. Discrimination (AUROC), calibration slope, Brier score, and decision curves were assessed with 1,500 bootstrap iterations.

Results: Naive single-source reporting captured only 25 recurrences in the paired cohort (n=187), missing 19 true recurrence events (43.2% of actual recurrences) documented longitudinally as new tumor events. These 19 missed cases were enriched for aggressive disease: 89.5% had pathologic T3/T4 stage, 63.2% had Gleason score 8 or higher, and mean Gleason was 8.16. Under naive unadjudicated endpoints, false-negative misclassification artificially suppressed clinical staging discrimination to AUROC 0.6368 (95% CI: 0.5067 to 0.7603), while adding the 13-gene panel generated a spurious incremental gain of delta AUROC +0.1168 (95% CI: +0.0229 to +0.2227). Following longitudinal harmonization, correct identification of all 19 events restored clinical staging discrimination to AUROC 0.7104 (95% CI: 0.6208 to 0.7913), completely eliminating apparent genomic advantage (delta AUROC -0.0076, 95% CI: -0.0763 to +0.0566). Decision curve analysis confirmed non-inferior clinical net benefit for clinical staging across all decision thresholds between 10% and 35%.

Conclusion: Apparent discrimination improvements from the 13-gene RNA panel in unadjudicated retrospective cohorts are artifacts of endpoint incompleteness, where missing aggressive recurrences artificially penalize clinical staging. Standardized clinical-pathologic staging provides robust, calibrated baseline prognostication for future AI and theranostic imaging triage.
