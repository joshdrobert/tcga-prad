### Abstract Body

#### Background
Public prostate cancer datasets contain heterogeneous recurrence definitions and incomplete molecular profiles, complicating the development and evaluation of prognostic models. We examined clinical endpoint concordance, missingness, and the incremental predictive value of transcriptomic measurements.

#### Methods
We analyzed 314 patients in the TCGA prostate adenocarcinoma cohort. Biochemical recurrence, new tumor events, and progression indicators were combined into an exploratory composite endpoint. We assessed outcome completeness, compared definitions against evaluable cBioPortal records, and examined five-fold out-of-fold model discrimination using clinical variables with and without a 13-gene expression panel.

#### Results
The composite endpoint identified 72 patients with documented recurrence or progression, including six without an ascertainable event date. Among 269 evaluable cBioPortal comparisons, 238 classifications agreed (88.5%), while 31 differed. Both primary recurrence indicators were documented negative for 191 patients; an additional 51 patients had incomplete or asymmetric outcome information. RNA expression profiles were available for 228 of 314 patients (72.6%). Within these 228 matched patients, clinical-only and clinical-plus-expression models achieved AUROCs of 0.689 and 0.686, respectively.

#### Conclusions
Endpoint discordance, uncertain recurrence ascertainment, and incomplete molecular coverage complicate prostate cancer prediction benchmarks. The selected expression panel showed no observed improvement in AUROC in this exploratory matched-cohort comparison. Further endpoint adjudication and independent validation are required.
