Recurrence Endpoint Ascertainment Reverses Apparent Genomic Advantage in Prostate Cancer Prognostication

PORTAL FIELD 4: ABSTRACT BODY (PASTE DIRECTLY INTO TEXT AREA)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Aim: Artificial intelligence models intended to inform postoperative prostate cancer recurrence risk may be sensitive to incomplete outcome ascertainment. We examined how biochemical recurrence (BCR) status alone versus multi-source longitudinal adjudication changes the apparent incremental discrimination of a 13-gene RNA panel over clinicopathologic staging.

Material & Methods: We evaluated 314 TCGA-PRAD radical prostatectomy cases and a matched complete-case molecular subcohort of 187 patients (44 harmonized recurrence events, 143 non-events). A narrower BCR-status-only outcome was compared with a multi-source composite definition integrating BCR, secondary new tumor events, and progression. Models included clinical staging (age, pathologic T and N stage, Gleason score; surgical margins excluded) and genomic models adding 13 transcripts. To guarantee fair comparison, models were evaluated across 5-fold cross-validation with frozen common patient-level fold assignments. Uncertainty was assessed using 1,500 paired patient-level bootstrap resamples for within-endpoint differences and the between-endpoint difference-in-differences. No PSMA-PET or treatment-response data were available.

Results: The narrower BCR-status-only outcome identified 25 events, leaving 19 additional composite recurrence events (43.2% of the 44 harmonized events) that were documented on longitudinal follow-up through secondary clinical records. Among these 19 discordant patients, 17 (89.5%) had pathologic T3/T4 stage and 12 (63.2%) had Gleason score 8 or higher (mean Gleason 8.16). Across frozen common folds, BCR-status-only modeling yielded clinical AUROC 0.6540 (95% CI: 0.5266 to 0.7721) versus 0.7457 (95% CI: 0.6197 to 0.8536) with RNA, an apparent incremental AUROC of +0.0917 (95% CI: +0.0001 to +0.1924). Following multi-source harmonization, clinical AUROC rose to 0.7069 (95% CI: 0.6197 to 0.7856) versus 0.6917 (95% CI: 0.5898 to 0.7865) with RNA, reversing incremental performance to -0.0153 (95% CI: -0.0861 to +0.0509). The resulting difference-in-differences was +0.1070 (95% CI: +0.0111 to +0.2013). Under outcome-dependent stratified folds, naive incremental AUROC was +0.1168 versus -0.0076 under harmonization.

Conclusion: Apparent incremental discrimination of this transcriptomic panel changed markedly with recurrence definition in identical participants. Incomplete endpoint ascertainment penalizes standard clinical staging by misclassifying aggressive recurrences. Auditable longitudinal outcomes are essential before prognostic AI algorithms can be validated for future theranostic triage.
